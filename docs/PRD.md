**Snooker Buddy — Product Requirements Document**

## 1. Overview

Snooker Buddy helps snooker coaches and their players organize exercises, assign training, and record results in one place.

The core workflow is **save an exercise → assign it → complete it → record and review results**. The MVP prioritizes easy saving of coaches’ existing exercises using photos of drawings.

## 2. Problem Statement

Exercise drawings get lost or mixed together, while verbal instructions are forgotten. During independent practice, players may substitute exercises they remember for those assigned. Without reliable completion records and results, coaches plan subsequent sessions using memory and subjective impressions.

The founder reports that approximately ten coaches independently identified these problems and that coaches and players want to participate in a pilot. Some coaches found existing applications expensive or unnecessarily complicated, particularly when saving exercises. The specific obstacles in that process still need to be observed.

## 3. Goal / Vision

Make preparation and follow-through straightforward: coaches maintain a reusable exercise collection, players understand their assignments, and both can review training history and performance on repeated exercises.

Simplicity is the intended differentiator, but its advantage over existing products remains to be validated.

## 4. Target Users

- **Coaches:** organize their own private exercises, prepare assignments, record joint sessions, and review player results.
- **Players working with a coach:** access assigned exercises and record independent practice.

Each coach can work with multiple players. Each player works with only one coach in the MVP.

## 5. Key Features (MVP)

- **Exercise library:** save a drawing photo, name, description, goal, difficulty label, and exercise type.
- **Search and filters:** find exercises by name, difficulty, and a shared predefined list of types.
- **Training assignments:** assign individual exercises or group exercises into a training session.
- **Personalized targets:** use default targets or adjust attempts and success criteria for a player.
- **Result recording:** capture successful versus total attempts, with optional consecutive-success and time measures.
- **Flexible entry:** record each attempt or enter a summary after the exercise.
- **Session ownership:** coaches record joint-session results; players record independent-session results.
- **Training history:** preserve separate performances for coaches and players to review completed work and results.

## 6. Out of Scope

- Automatic player categorization or promotion between skill levels.
- Standardized progression criteria.
- Multiple coaches working with the same player.
- Sharing exercise libraries between coaches.
- A digital snooker-table editor.
- Coach-defined exercise types.

Custom development on demand is not a product commitment. Difficulty labels organize exercises; they do not establish validated player levels.

## 7. Success Metrics

The primary outcome is an organized exercise collection and straightforward training preparation. Pilot indicators will include:

| Indicator | Measure |
|---|---|
| Exercise saving | Completion rate and time taken to save an exercise. |
| Exercise retrieval | Success rate and time taken to find a needed exercise. |
| Training preparation | Time to prepare and assign training; frequency of reconstructing lost instructions. |
| Independent practice clarity | Share of observed assignments understood and repeated without extra clarification. |
| Result recording | Share of performed exercises with recorded results, assessed against pilot participants’ training logs or feedback. |
| History usefulness | Frequency with which coaches report consulting history when preparing training. |
| Continued use | Weekly active coaches and players, retention through the pilot, and stated intention to continue. |

Targets and pilot duration are not yet agreed. Better exercise scores alone do not demonstrate improved overall playing ability.

## 8. Risks and Assumptions

- Interview findings and pilot commitments are founder-reported; regular use remains unproven.
- Players do not currently track attempts, so consistent and accurate recording requires a new habit.
- Existing products overlap with the concept; easier exercise saving must provide a meaningful advantage.
- Supporting multiple entry modes and optional measures could undermine simplicity.
- Difficulty labels are subjective and should not imply comparability between coaches.
- Willingness to pay and a sustainable price remain unvalidated.

## 9. Open Questions

- What are the pilot duration, participant count, and numerical targets for each success indicator?
- How will baseline performance and pilot indicators be measured consistently?
- What exactly makes saving exercises difficult in the applications coaches have tried?
- What difficulty scale and final predefined exercise-type list should be used?
- How should consecutive-success and time-based results be defined?
- Who would pay, how much, and what evidence would justify further investment?
